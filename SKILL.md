# PptSkill

A specialized skill for creating high-quality academic presentations, especially graduate-level paper presentation slides.

This skill guides agents to transform academic papers into visually appealing, logically structured, and professionally designed presentations suitable for research group meetings, seminars, and academic discussions.

The primary goal is not simply generating slides, but creating presentations that feel carefully designed by a human researcher rather than automatically generated by AI.

---

## Goals

### 1. Create Natural and Human-designed Presentations

- Avoid obvious AI-generated presentation styles.
- Avoid excessive text, repetitive layouts, and mechanical page structures.
- Ensure every slide has a clear communication purpose.
- Prioritize information hierarchy and storytelling over content accumulation.

### 2. Maintain Academic Presentation Quality

- Follow the conventions of graduate research group presentations.
- Clearly explain research motivation, methodology, experiments, and contributions.
- Preserve academic rigor while improving readability.
- Highlight important technical insights instead of simply copying paper content.

### 3. Achieve Professional Visual Design

- Use clean and balanced layouts.
- Maintain consistent typography, spacing, and alignment.
- Use appropriate visual elements including diagrams, charts, figures, and illustrations.
- Ensure sufficient white space to improve readability.

### 4. Provide Fresh and Comfortable Visual Style

- Use modern but restrained color palettes.
- Prefer clean academic styles over flashy business presentation styles.
- Maintain visual consistency across all slides.
- Avoid excessive gradients, shadows, decorative elements, and unnecessary animations.

### 5. Improve Information Communication

- Convert dense academic text into understandable visual structures.
- Use diagrams, flowcharts, comparison tables, and conceptual illustrations when appropriate.
- Ensure the audience can understand the main ideas within a short presentation time.

---

# Use When

Use this skill when:

- The user provides a research paper and requests a presentation.
- The user needs to prepare a graduate research group meeting presentation.
- The user asks for a paper summary PPT.
- The user wants a conference-style academic presentation.
- The user requests improving the visual quality or layout of an existing academic PPT.
- The user wants a professional research presentation without an obvious AI-generated style.

---

# Do Not Use When

Do not use this skill when:

- The user only wants a simple text summary of a paper.
- The user requests business presentations, marketing slides, or commercial pitches.
- The user only needs spelling correction or translation.
- The user requests artistic posters rather than academic presentations.

# Workflow

## Step 1: Understand the Presentation Objective

Before designing slides:

- Identify the presentation scenario:
  - Graduate group meeting.
  - Course presentation.
  - Conference presentation.
  - Research discussion.

- Determine:
  - Expected presentation duration.
  - Audience background.
  - Required technical depth.
  - Number of slides.

If important information is missing, ask targeted questions.

---

## Step 2: Analyze the Paper Content

Read and extract the core structure of the paper:

- Research background.
- Motivation.
- Problem definition.
- Research gap.
- Main contributions.
- Proposed method.
- Technical framework.
- Experimental setup.
- Main results.
- Limitations.
- Future directions.

Do not directly copy paragraphs from the paper.

Instead:

- Extract key ideas.
- Simplify explanations.
- Build a logical narrative.

---

## Step 3: Design Presentation Storyline

Construct the overall presentation flow.

Default academic paper presentation structure:

1. Title Page
   - Paper title.
   - Authors.
   - Venue/year.
   - Presenter information.

2. Background and Motivation
   - Why this problem matters.
   - Current challenges.
   - Research motivation.

3. Related Work
   - Existing approaches.
   - Limitations of previous methods.

4. Research Problem
   - Formal problem definition.
   - Key challenges.

5. Proposed Method
   - Overall framework.
   - Core ideas.
   - Technical details.

6. Experiments
   - Dataset.
   - Experimental settings.
   - Baselines.
   - Evaluation metrics.

7. Results Analysis
   - Main results.
   - Important observations.
   - Ablation studies.

8. Limitations and Discussion
   - Paper weaknesses.
   - Possible improvements.

9. Conclusion
   - Main contributions.
   - Key takeaways.

Adjust the structure according to paper complexity.

---

## Step 4: Design Individual Slides

For each slide:

Define:

- Slide purpose.
- Main message.
- Visual focus.
- Required elements.

Each slide should answer:

"What is the one key point the audience should remember?"

Avoid:

- Long paragraphs.
- More than 5-7 bullet points.
- Copying paper sections directly.
- Text-heavy explanations.

Prefer:

- One main idea per slide.
- Short phrases instead of sentences.
- Visual explanations instead of descriptions.

---

## Step 5: Apply Visual Design Principles

### Layout

Use:

- Balanced margins.
- Clear alignment.
- Strong visual hierarchy.
- Appropriate whitespace.

Avoid:

- Crowded layouts.
- Random element placement.
- Excessive boxes.

---

### Typography

Use:

- Clear title hierarchy.
- Consistent font sizes.
- Limited font families.

Recommended:

- Large concise titles.
- Medium-sized section headings.
- Readable body text.

Avoid:

- Too-small text.
- Excessive bold formatting.
- Multiple decorative fonts.

---

### Color System

Use:

- 1 primary color.
- 1-2 supporting colors.
- Neutral background colors.

Preferred styles:

- Academic blue.
- Soft green.
- Clean gray.
- Minimal black-white combinations.

Avoid:

- Neon colors.
- Strong gradients.
- Excessive color usage.

---

### Visual Elements

Prefer:

- Paper framework diagrams.
- Method pipelines.
- Comparison charts.
- Concept maps.
- Experimental result visualization.

Avoid:

- Generic stock illustrations.
- Decorative icons without meaning.
- AI-generated-looking graphics.

---

## Step 6: Reduce AI-generated Appearance

Before finalizing:

Check:

- Does every slide have a clear purpose?
- Is the layout varied naturally?
- Are there unnecessary decorative elements?
- Does the presentation look like a researcher prepared it manually?
- Is the text concise enough for oral presentation?

Avoid common AI PPT patterns:

- Every slide having identical structure.
- Huge titles with empty content.
- Excessive bullet lists.
- Generic phrases such as "In conclusion, this innovative method..."
- Overly polished but meaningless decoration.

---

## Step 7: Final Quality Review

Before delivering the PPT:

Check:

### Content

- Is the research logic complete?
- Are technical details accurate?
- Are important contributions highlighted?

### Visual Design

- Is the layout consistent?
- Is the typography readable?
- Are colors harmonious?
- Are figures understandable?

### Presentation Experience

- Can the presenter explain each slide naturally?
- Is the information density appropriate?
- Does the audience easily follow the research story?

---

# Output Requirements

When generating a presentation:

Provide:

1. Slide-by-slide structure.
2. Each slide's title.
3. Key content points.
4. Recommended layout.
5. Suggested diagrams or visual elements.
6. Speaker notes when necessary.

The final PPT should be:

- Academic.
- Clean.
- Professional.
- Human-designed.
- Suitable for graduate research seminars.
